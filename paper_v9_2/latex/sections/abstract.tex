\begin{abstract}
Generative AI can make local software implementation faster, but it may also
raise the importance of shared architectural intent, integration, and human
coordination. This study examines that tension across two cohorts of student
software projects comprising 14 teams and three synchronized checkpoints
($T_1$/$T_2$/$T_3$). We combine multi-checkpoint surveys, repository mining,
independent blind-scored evaluator scorecards, and transcript-based evidence to
study Generative AI adoption and role perceptions, the temporal density of
repository activity, and associations between upfront architectural artifacts,
late rework, and project outcomes. The results show that self-reported AI use
is widespread and task-oriented, but perception families do not form a single
longitudinal trajectory. Repository activity and clean source/test change
concentrate near final integration, but internal contrasts show that this late
concentration includes cases consistent with compensatory recovery, cases
without evaluator-score gain, and cases where improvement occurs with more
distributed activity. Candidate coordination-language density increases across
the observed transcript checkpoints, while observable T1 architectural-artifact
scope varies substantially without a simple monotonic association with T3
clean-rework magnitude, baseline-conditioned rework ratios, or independent
outcomes. Robustness checks using non-overlapping phases,
operational-regularity proxies, influence diagnostics, complexity profiles, and
explicit transcript coverage make the main validity threats inspectable but do
not remove the study's observational limits. These findings suggest that Agile
remains necessary, but AI-assisted development requires a more explicit and
inspectable layer of architectural planning, interfaces, dependencies, and
acceptance conditions. Spec-Driven Development is discussed as a plausible,
testable response to the coordination gap exposed by the combined timing,
planning, rework, complexity, and influence diagnostics.
\end{abstract}
