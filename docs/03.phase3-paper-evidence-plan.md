# Phase 3 Plan: Paper Evidence, Literature, and Argumentation

**Status:** planned, based on the frozen Phase 2 artifact set and the generated Phase 3 evidence inventory

**Target submission:** ICSE 2027 Software Engineering Education and Training (SEET), **Research Paper** category.

**Submission constraints currently published by SEET 2027:** maximum 10 pages plus up to 2 pages of references, IEEE conference proceedings format, double-anonymous review, and a required data-availability statement after the Conclusion. The published call accepts negative and mixed findings and evaluates research papers on relevance, significance, soundness, verifiability, and presentation. The submission deadline is 23 October 2026, 23:59 AoE. These details must be rechecked before submission because the call may change.

## 1. Purpose

Phase 3 converts the persisted Phase 2 evidence into a paper-ready argument. It does not treat the current dataset as proof of the central thesis. It identifies and organizes the strongest signals compatible with the narrative arc, makes their provenance and denominators inspectable, and prepares the limitations and null findings for an explicit **Threats to Validity** section.

The working thesis remains intentionally qualified:

> In generative-AI-supported software engineering education, insufficiently explicit planning may be associated with planning debt, rework, and coordination friction; the current evidence motivates SDD/BDUF as a methodological proposal, but does not establish it as the only sustainable solution.

The paper must distinguish three levels of statement:

1. **Observed:** directly reported by a persisted artifact.
2. **Interpretive:** a bounded explanation consistent with the observed pattern and the literature.
3. **Proposed:** a methodological implication or future hypothesis about BDUF/SDD.

No proposed or interpretive statement may be written as if it were an observed causal result.

### 2.1 Permitted exception: separately authorized derived analysis

A new analysis is allowed only when a concrete paper requirement cannot be met from the persisted artifacts. Before execution, it must have:

- a separate script under a clearly named Phase 3 analysis location;
- a written justification identifying the missing claim or denominator;
- declared inputs, filters, unit of analysis, and output contract;
- a statement that it does not mutate or replace Phase 2 artifacts;
- explicit user authorization before execution.

A proposal for such an analysis must be presented first. No exploratory rerun or untracked notebook calculation is part of the baseline Phase 3 plan.

## 3. Narrative-to-paper strategy

The formal paper structure will be **IMRaD plus Threats to Validity and Data Availability**, while the four acts provide the argument map within and across those sections.

| Narrative act | Main paper role | Evidence emphasis | Permitted claim strength |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Act 1: evolutionary ceiling | Introduction and motivation | Generative-AI context, planning complexity, educational setting | Conceptual and contextual |
| Act 2: planning debt | Results and Discussion | Planning metrics, code churn, technical degradation, rework-related signals | Descriptive and exploratory |
| Act 3: human factor | Results and Discussion | Integration friction, author concentration, temporal/cut-context signals, student/transcript NLP at its supported granularity | Descriptive and interpretive |
| Act 4: value inversion | Discussion and implications | Cross-act synthesis and literature; SDD/BDUF as a proposal | Implication, not empirical proof |

The arc must be used to organize evidence, not to force every artifact to support the thesis. An artifact may be marked as supporting a narrow descriptive claim, inconclusive, unavailable, or relevant only as a limitation.

### 3.1 Report-first reading protocol

For every candidate paper claim, use this order:

1. Read the relevant Phase 2.5 artifact report.
2. Follow its narrative binding to the corresponding Phase 2.5 act report.
3. Check the consolidated Phase 2.5 audit and its verdict.
4. If the claim uses cross-evidence, read the corresponding cross-evidence
   artifact, group, act, and consolidated reports.
5. Verify the claim against the source Parquet/CSV/JSON, sidecar, and figure
   manifest.
6. Record the strongest wording allowed by all layers, using the most
   conservative status when they disagree.

The report is therefore the starting point for interpretation, but never the
sole source of a number. A report that omits a p-value or limitation does not
license the paper to omit it when the underlying table or exclusion manifest
contains it.

## 4. Workstreams and deliverables

All Phase 3 support artifacts belong under `data/analysis/paper_support/`. Interpretation, bibliography notes, and the manuscript outline belong under `docs/phase3/` unless a later task explicitly changes that convention.

### 4.1 Workstream A: Evidence inventory and data book

**Deliverable:** `data/analysis/paper_support/data_book.md`

Treat `phase3_evidence_inventory.md` as the initial evidence map, then create a
reproducible, paper-facing data book containing one entry per candidate metric,
dataset, test, report, and selected figure. Each entry must include:

- artifact path and sidecar path;
- producer script and contract version;
- unit of analysis and observation key;
- row count, unique-key count, coverage, and missingness;
- exact variables and transformations used in the paper;
- numerator, denominator, and aggregation rule where applicable;
- relevant exclusions and unavailable reasons;
- the supported narrative act and paper section;
- the strongest statement the artifact permits;
- the statement it does **not** permit;
- status: `observed`, `interpretive`, `proposed`, `inconclusive`, or `unavailable`.
- evidence layer: `phase2.5_artifact_report`, `phase2.5_act_report`,
   `phase2.5_consolidated`, `cross_evidence_report`, `structured_source`, or
   `figure_manifest`;
- reconciliation status: `consistent`, `qualified`, `conflict`, or
   `not_applicable`.

The data book must link to source artifacts rather than copy large tables. Every number quoted in the manuscript must be traceable to an entry in this book and, from there, to a persisted source file.

### 4.2 Workstream B: Claim-evidence-reference matrix

**Deliverable:** `data/analysis/paper_support/argument_matrix.csv` and a readable companion `docs/phase3/argument-matrix.md`

The matrix is the central control document for the paper. Each row represents one claim and must contain:

- `claim_id`;
- IMRaD section and narrative act;
- claim text;
- claim type: contextual, methodological, descriptive, interpretive, or proposed;
- metric/dataset and unit of analysis;
- source artifact and figure/table identifier;
- exact n, denominator, missingness, and statistical status;
- compatible result, null result, or contradiction;
- candidate reference keys;
- confidence label: `high`, `moderate`, `exploratory`, or `not_supported`;
- threat-to-validity note;
- manuscript paragraph destination;
- decision: `retain`, `qualify`, `move_to_limitations`, or `drop`.
- interpretive authority: the exact Phase 2.5 report path and section that
   first frames the claim;
- numeric verification: the exact Parquet/CSV/JSON or figure-manifest path;
- cross-evidence role, when applicable: `candidate_support`, `context`,
   `fragility`, `methodological_warning`, or `not_used`.

The matrix must prevent narrative drift: an attractive plot without a defensible claim is a candidate visual, not evidence for the thesis.

### 4.3 Workstream C: Plot book and figure selection

**Deliverable:** `data/analysis/paper_support/plot_book.md` and `data/analysis/paper_support/selected_figures.json`

Review all persisted JPG/PNG/HTML/CSV-backed figures listed by
`figure_manifest.json`, the cross-evidence figure manifests, and the files in
`assets/figures/`. The first selection pass may prioritize visual clarity and
narrative usefulness, as requested, but a figure can enter the paper set only
after its audit record is complete and its Phase 2.5 or cross-evidence report
binding is recorded.

Each plot-book entry must document:

- figure identifier, source artifact, and physical path;
- intended paper section and narrative act;
- visual question answered;
- x/y variables, transformations, and aggregation;
- n, denominator, and missing observations;
- whether the visual is descriptive, inferential, or illustrative;
- caption draft with a non-causal interpretation;
- limitations and exclusions;
- accessibility/readability checks for IEEE page size;
- double-anonymous metadata check;
- decision: `paper`, `supplementary`, or `archive`.
- report-first interpretation path and whether the figure agrees with the
   consolidated audit;
- for cross-evidence figures, leave-one-out/semester-stratified status and
   relevant exclusion classes.

The plot book must explicitly reject plots that imply causal direction, hide small samples, mix units of analysis, or visually convert an unavailable/inconclusive result into a positive finding.

The initial cross-evidence visual review must cover the seven prioritized
families listed in Section 2. For the paper, the default candidates are
`scope_vs_late_instability`, `temporal_escalation_panel`, and
`author_pressure_vs_churn`; `file_category_churn_by_cut` is conditional on an
explicit treatment of its exclusion burden, and
`leave_one_out_robustness` must be used as a qualification visual rather than
as positive evidence.

### 4.4 Workstream D: Literature and BibTeX

**Deliverables:**

- `docs/phase3/references.bib`
- `docs/phase3/literature-map.md`
- `data/analysis/paper_support/reference_matrix.csv`

Use a relevance-based search with no rigid publication-year cutoff. Prioritize recent work, but include foundational sources where needed for Agile, BDUF, technical debt, software engineering education, teamwork, coordination, code churn/rework, AI-assisted programming, and specification-driven development.

Each reference must be classified by function:

- `contextual`: establishes the educational or generative-AI setting;
- `theoretical`: defines planning debt, technical debt, coordination, cognition, or related constructs;
- `methodological`: supports the measurement or analysis choice;
- `empirical_external`: provides external evidence comparable to this study;
- `pedagogical`: connects the study to SEET’s education and training audience;
- `counterpoint`: challenges or bounds the thesis.

The literature map should favor references that help explain the observed evidence. It must not cite a paper as proof of this dataset’s result. The reference matrix must link each retained claim to one or more BibTeX keys and record whether the reference supports, contextualizes, or qualifies the claim.

### 4.5 Workstream E: Threats to validity and null findings

**Deliverable:** `docs/phase3/threats_to_validity.md`

Create a dedicated record for:

- small and uneven samples;
- non-significant primary tests;
- unavailable or zero-variance analyses;
- observational, non-causal design;
- multiple granularities and non-paired transcript evidence;
- limitations in mapping educational observations to software engineering practice;
- possible construct validity issues for PI, CC, AI, DeltaDT, and IE;
- selection and survivorship effects in repositories;
- privacy-driven restrictions on sharing raw data;
- any discrepancy between a visual pattern and its statistical test.
- report/source conflicts, including reports that omit p-values or describe an
   exploratory artifact as support without carrying the associated caveat;
- cross-evidence multiplicity: seven correlations, 21 semester-stratified
   rows, 24 best/worst contrasts, seven leave-one-out rows, and 18 extreme-case
   overlap rows must not be narrated as independent confirmatory tests;
- the difference between the cross-evidence aggregate verdict (`supports`) and
   its many inconclusive/fragility entries;
- category-classification exclusions and their effect on denominators;
- the absence of empirical support for Acts 1 and 4 in the cross-evidence act
   reports, which remain contextual/interpretive rather than data-proven.

The paper may mention these threats in Results where they change interpretation, but the complete treatment belongs in this deliverable and in the manuscript’s Threats to Validity section. Null findings are part of the evidence record, not evidence in favor of the thesis.

### 4.6 Workstream F: IMRaD manuscript skeleton

**Deliverable:** `docs/phase3/manuscript-outline.md`

Prepare an outline compatible with the SEET Research Paper limit:

1. **Introduction**
   - educational and generative-AI context;
   - problem statement and motivation;
   - qualified planning-debt hypothesis;
   - research questions;
   - contributions stated as empirical characterization, evidence organization, and methodological implications, not proof of SDD superiority.
2. **Background and Related Work**
   - Agile and planning;
   - planning/technical debt and rework;
   - AI-assisted software engineering education;
   - coordination, teamwork, and software engineering skills;
   - SDD/BDUF as the proposed response.
3. **Method**
   - educational context and participants/teams at the permitted disclosure level;
   - data sources and ethical/privacy treatment;
   - unit of analysis by artifact;
   - metric definitions and temporal cuts;
   - analysis procedures and missingness handling;
   - reproducibility and artifact lineage.
4. **Results**
   - descriptive dataset profile;
   - report-first evidence map: what Phase 2.5 says before source verification;
   - planning debt and code churn evidence;
   - integration and human-factor evidence;
   - clearly separated secondary cross-evidence candidates and robustness
     checks;
   - plots selected from the plot book;
   - explicit reporting of null, unavailable, and mixed results.
5. **Discussion**
   - interpretation organized by the four acts;
   - reconciliation of the Phase 2.5 conditional-go verdict with the
     cross-evidence exploratory supports;
   - comparison with the literature;
   - implications for SE education and training;
   - SDD/BDUF as a qualified methodological proposal;
   - actionable lessons that do not exceed the evidence.
6. **Threats to Validity**
   - the complete limitations record from Workstream E;
   - scope and generalizability;
   - causal and statistical limitations.
7. **Conclusion**
   - concise answer to the research questions;
   - what the data supports now;
   - what requires future evaluation.
8. **Data Availability**
   - what anonymized artifacts can be released;
   - what cannot be released and why;
   - how reviewers can verify the persisted analysis without exposing PII.

The Results section must not present the cross-evidence aggregate verdict as a
replacement for the Phase 2.5 conditional-go verdict. It should present
cross-evidence as exploratory triangulation that identifies candidate patterns
for future testing.

Every subsection in the outline must link to claim IDs, selected figures/tables, and reference keys.

## 5. Evidence-selection protocol

Use the following sequence for each candidate claim:

1. Start from the relevant Phase 2.5 Markdown report and capture its act
   binding, status, and stated limitations.
2. Check the Phase 2.5 act report and consolidated audit.
3. If cross-evidence is involved, check artifact, group, act, and consolidated
   cross-evidence reports, including support and inconclusive entries.
4. Locate the source artifact and successful sidecar.
5. Confirm its unit of analysis, denominator, coverage, exclusions, and
   transformations in the data book.
6. Check the statistical result, if any, in the relevant source CSV or JSON;
   do not rely on a report's summary when the source contains more precise
   values.
7. Classify the claim as observed, interpretive, or proposed.
8. Select the clearest visual only after the underlying numbers are recorded.
9. Add a reference that explains or contextualizes the finding, not one that
   substitutes for evidence.
10. Record the strongest allowed wording and the required qualification.
11. Add the claim to the manuscript outline and the relevant act package.

A claim is eligible for the main Results section only when its source, n, denominator, and limitation are known. A visually persuasive pattern with an unavailable or non-significant test may still be reported as exploratory, but it cannot be described as confirmation.

Claims based only on cross-evidence require an additional label of
`secondary exploratory evidence` and must cite the corresponding report and
structured source row. A favorable p-value is not sufficient when the claim is
part of a multiple exploratory family, is unstable under leave-one-out
analysis, or depends on heavily excluded categories.

## 7. SEET-specific quality gates

Before the Phase 3 deliverables are considered complete, verify:

- the paper is framed as a Research Paper about software engineering education/training, not only as a general critique of Agile;
- the educational context, learning/training relevance, and actionable implications are explicit;
- the outline fits 10 pages plus 2 reference pages in IEEE format;
- figures remain legible at the final page size and have captions that state unit and n;
- double-anonymous review is preserved in manuscript, figures, artifact links, repository metadata, and supplementary material;
- no raw PII, private evidence fields, author names, repository identity, or identifying URLs leak into paper-support artifacts intended for review;
- the Data Availability section is planned after the Conclusion;
- every empirical claim has a source path and every literature claim has a BibTeX key;
- mixed and null findings remain visible;
- the Phase 2.5 reports are the interpretive entry point for every empirical
   claim;
- cross-evidence supports are explicitly marked as secondary exploratory
   candidates and are reconciled with inconclusive and robustness outputs;
- the 27-artifact cross-evidence manifest and its four exclusion classes are
   represented in the data book;
- proposed SDD/BDUF implications are not presented as experimentally established superiority.
