\subsection{The Agile value hierarchy and coordination risk}

The Agile Manifesto prioritizes working software, interaction, and response to
change over comprehensive documentation~\cite{agile_manifesto_2001}. This
emphasis remains valuable when requirements evolve, but it does not imply that
shared architectural intent is unnecessary. Classic accounts of software
coordination emphasize that communication costs grow with system size and team
interaction, especially when structure remains tacit~\cite{brooks_1975_mythical_man_month,demarco_lister_1987_peopleware}.
The relevant question for AI-assisted development is therefore not whether
teams should return to immutable upfront design, but which forms of intent must
remain explicit while implementation continues iteratively.

\subsection{Generative AI in software engineering: From local generation to systems}

Recent work presents Generative AI as a major change in software-engineering
practice while emphasizing that its effects vary across tasks, users, and
organizational contexts~\cite{carleton2024generative,banh2025copiloting,russo2024navigating}.
Empirical studies of code-generating tools show that programmers work through
iterative interaction, verification, and contextual steering rather than
simply accepting generated code~\cite{barke2023grounded}. This distinction is
central to the present study: local code generation may accelerate an activity
without resolving cross-file dependencies, architectural boundaries, or team
coordination. Software-engineering education is already confronting this
change, including questions about how students use and assess Generative AI in
project work~\cite{yabaku2024usage}.

\subsection{Upfront planning, architecture, and Spec-Driven Development}

Architecture and technical-debt research treats design decisions as evolving
constraints whose consequences may accumulate over time. Architectural debt
research has developed concepts and tools for identifying architectural debt,
following its accumulation, and relating it to later remediation
effort~\cite{verdecchia2018architectural,nayebi2019longitudinal}.
These traditions motivate measuring early architectural artifacts separately
from semantic quality: an observed artifact is evidence of an observable
practice, not proof that its design was adequate.

Spec-Driven Development (SDD) extends this concern into AI-assisted workflows.
Current practitioner descriptions frame SDD as carrying a specification through
planning, task decomposition, implementation, and convergence checks
\cite{github2025spec,thoughtworks202Xspec}. In this paper, SDD is treated as a
testable process proposition: making intent and constraints inspectable may
reduce ambiguity when AI tools generate implementation across multiple files
and contributors. Existing SDD guidance is used to define the proposition, not
as evidence that SDD improves outcomes.

\subsection{Technical debt, rework, and empirical measurement}

Technical debt describes the future cost of expedient decisions, but its
measurement is difficult because productive iteration can resemble rework in
volume-based repository data~\cite{cunningham_1992_technical_debt,fowler2009technicaldebt,kruchten2012technicaldebt}.
Architecture-debt studies likewise distinguish observable signals from claims
about design quality or defect causation. The present work follows that
measurement discipline by separating activity volume, clean-path provenance,
baseline eligibility, and evaluator outcomes rather than collapsing them into a
single score.

\subsection{Research gap and contribution}

The related literature provides strong accounts of Agile coordination, GenAI
adoption and interaction, architectural debt, and evolving software practice.
What remains less visible is how these perspectives meet in student projects
that use Generative AI while being observed across synchronized checkpoints.
This study addresses that gap through a bounded mixed-method design combining
survey reports, repository activity and path provenance, independent evaluator
scorecards, and transcript-based coordination evidence. Its contribution is
not a causal estimate of AI productivity or a validation of SDD. It is an
evidence-preserving account of how local AI-assisted implementation, temporal
integration pressure, explicit architectural artifacts, rework proxies, and
independent outcomes can be examined together without silently merging their
analytical grains.
