\section{Background and Related Work}

Agile development prioritizes working software, interaction, and response to
change over comprehensive documentation~\cite{agile_manifesto_2001}, but that
value hierarchy does not remove the need for shared architectural intent.
Classic coordination accounts emphasize that communication costs grow with
system size and team interaction when structure remains tacit
~\cite{brooks_1975_mythical_man_month,demarco_lister_1987_peopleware}. The
GenAI-era question is therefore not whether teams should return to immutable
upfront design, but which intent, interface, dependency, and acceptance
constraints must remain explicit while implementation iterates.

Generative AI changes local software-engineering work unevenly across tasks,
users, and contexts~\cite{carleton2024generative,banh2025copiloting,russo2024navigating}.
Studies of code-generating tools show that developers steer, verify, and
contextualize generated code rather than merely accepting it~\cite{barke2023grounded}.
For student projects, this distinction matters because faster local generation
does not automatically resolve cross-file dependencies, architectural
boundaries, or coordination across contributors. Software-engineering
education is already confronting these questions as students adopt GenAI in
project work~\cite{yabaku2024usage}.

Architecture and technical-debt research similarly treats design decisions as
evolving constraints whose consequences may accumulate over time
~\cite{cunningham_1992_technical_debt,fowler2009technicaldebt,kruchten2012technicaldebt,verdecchia2018architectural,nayebi2019longitudinal}.
This motivates measuring early architectural artifacts separately from semantic
quality: an observed artifact is evidence of an observable practice, not proof
that the design was adequate. Spec-Driven Development (SDD) extends this
concern into AI-assisted workflows by carrying a specification through
planning, task decomposition, implementation, and convergence checks
~\cite{github2025spec,thoughtworks202Xspec}. We treat SDD as a testable
process proposition, not as an already validated intervention.

The gap addressed here is how these perspectives meet in student projects that
use GenAI while being observed across synchronized checkpoints. The study
combines survey reports, repository activity and path provenance, independent
evaluator scorecards, and transcript-based coordination evidence. Its
contribution is not a causal estimate of AI productivity or a validation of
SDD; it is an evidence-preserving account of how local AI-assisted
implementation, temporal integration pressure, explicit artifacts, rework
proxies, and independent outcomes can be examined together without merging
their analytical grains.
