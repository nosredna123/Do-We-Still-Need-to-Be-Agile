\section{Results}
This section reports the descriptive and exploratory results produced by the
validated metric artifacts. Results are organized by research question and
retain the analytical grain of each metric; no claim combines incompatible
grains or treats self-reported evidence as behavioral telemetry.

\subsection{RQ1: How do student developers utilize Generative AI, and how does this adoption shape their longitudinal perceptions of software engineering roles and project expectations?}

RQ1 is supported by two distinct evidence streams: Self-Reported AI Usage and
Perceptions (M1),
which summarizes six perception families and separately reports self-reported
AI use, and Perceived Role Disruption (M2), which summarizes perceived
disruption agreement for six software-engineering roles. All six
cohort-by-semester/checkpoint cells in the M1 family
have complete item coverage, with 46, 41, and 40 respondents in 2025.2 and
22, 20, and 18 respondents in 2026.1 at $T_1$, $T_2$, and $T_3$, respectively.
These are repeated cohort summaries, not an identified individual longitudinal
panel.
% PROVENANCE: M1 values and coverage are from
% paper_v9/data/metrics/m1_rq1_perception_panel_long.csv and
% paper_v9/data/metrics/m1_rq1_perception_panel.metadata.json. The executed
% verification notebook is paper_v9/verification_notebooks/verify_m1.ipynb.

The six perception families do not move as a single trajectory. Mean AI
benefit increases in 2025.2 but declines in 2026.1, while autonomy/tool

not be interpreted as one latent measure of AI dependence. Figure~\ref{fig:rq1-m1}
presents these six families with cohort-specific standard-deviation bars; exact
endpoint means and standard deviations are available in the replication package.

directions rather than showing a uniform displacement trajectory. Agreement is
therefore role-specific and cohort-dependent; it does not support a general
claim that all roles become progressively more exposed to replacement or
transformation.

\subsection{RQ2: How does the temporal density of repository activity contrast with the qualitative typification of human coordination friction across the project lifecycle?}

RQ2 contrasts three descriptive evidence streams that operate at different
grains. Repository Activity (M3) measures activity timing, author participation,
and concentration for 14 team-semesters. Clean-Change Dynamics (M4) measures
clean source/test change at team-semester and checkpoint grain.
Coordination-Language Evidence (M5) counts candidate coordination markers
within a global transcript corpus by checkpoint. The measures are therefore
reported side by side as complementary descriptions, not as a common-scale
effect or a statistical correlation.
% PROVENANCE: RQ2 coverage and grains are declared in
% paper_v9/data/metrics/m3_author_activity_dynamics.metadata.json,
% m4_clean_change_dynamics.metadata.json, and
% m5_coordination_evidence.metadata.json.

Repository Activity (M3) shows a pronounced concentration around the final
evaluator anchor. The semester-pooled rolling view rises toward the final
checkpoint, peaks immediately after it, and then falls sharply, producing a
clear late-stage bottleneck. These rolling windows overlap, so adjacent points
are temporal summaries rather than independent observations. Figure~\ref{fig:rq2-m3}
visualizes this pooled temporal shape, and Table~\ref{tab:rq2-summary} reports
the checkpoint values and units.

\caption{Repository Activity (M3) in pooled rolling windows. The vertical marker identifies
the $T_3$ evaluator anchor; the annotation identifies the pooled peak.}
\label{fig:rq2-m3}
\end{figure*}
% PROVENANCE: Values are from
% paper_v9/data/metrics/m3_activity_rolling_7day_pooled.csv. The M3

The checkpoint participation view shows the same concentration with substantial
heterogeneity. Across the 14 team-semesters, 7 have any post-checkpoint
activity. The final-window share of all commits ranges from $9.5\%$ to
$100.0\%$; three team-semesters have fewer than ten total commits, and their
shares should be treated as small-denominator descriptions. In the author
concentration output, the median pre-anchor window contains 12.5 commits,
3.5 active days, and 3 authors, with a median maximum-author share of $0.50$
and median Gini coefficient of $0.22$. Here, the Gini coefficient summarizes
how unevenly commits are distributed across authors, with values near zero
indicating more even participation and larger values indicating stronger
author-level concentration. Post-anchor windows are frequently inactive; where
activity exists, concentration statistics are not interpreted for no-activity
cases.
% PROVENANCE: Checkpoint and author summaries are from
% paper_v9/data/metrics/m3_author_activity_participation.csv and
% m3_author_concentration.csv. No author identity is exposed in these outputs.

The same final-stage pattern remains visible when the 91 days before $T_3$ are
re-expressed as mutually exclusive phases rather than overlapping rolling
windows. These phase-share summaries are retained in the replication artifacts
as descriptive checks against the overlap structure of the rolling M3 view, not
as independent causal tests.
% PROVENANCE: Non-overlapping phase shares are from
% paper_v9/figures/rq2_phase_activity_share_summary.csv and
% rq2_nonoverlapping_phase_activity.metadata.json.

To address whether the final peak is simply ``student syndrome,'' we compare
final-seven-day concentration--the share of a team's total repository activity
occurring in the seven days around final delivery--with evaluator-score change
and repository-visible planning scope at team-semester grain ($n=14$). Higher
final concentration appears in both positive-delta and non-positive-delta
cases, and the clean-churn variant agrees directionally with the commit-based
view. Figure~\ref{fig:rq2-planning-commit} keeps the representative
planning/concentration contrast in the main text; the score-delta and
clean-churn variants remain in the replication artifacts.

Operational regularity provides a final repository-visible process check. The
regularity index is descriptive, with larger values indicating activity more
distributed across the observed Git timeline; it is not a validated agile
process scale. Both low and high final concentration appear across the observed
regularity range, so the late-activity interpretation remains bounded to Git
evidence rather than treated as a direct measure of team process maturity.

Clean-Change Dynamics (M4) reproduces the temporal increase using a narrower
clean-change definition.
Pooled clean churn rises from $21{,}718$ lines across 7 observed team-semesters
at $T_1$, to $47{,}545$ lines across 10 at $T_2$, and $136{,}325$ lines across
all 14 at $T_3$. The median clean churn per team-semester rises from $66.5$
lines at $T_1$ to $3{,}232.5$ at $T_2$ and $6{,}975.5$ at $T_3$. Clean change
accounts for $42.9\%$, $19.6\%$, and $1.0\%$ of all recorded churn at the
three checkpoints, respectively, because excluded or non-measurement paths
dominate the raw line-change total in the final checkpoint. This distinction is
why Clean-Change Dynamics (M4) is not interchangeable with total repository
activity.

Coordination-Language Evidence (M5) provides a separate textual axis for the
2025.2 transcript corpus. The
corpus contains 35, 37, and 28 transcript chunks at $T_1$, $T_2$, and $T_3$,

density increases from $0.515$ to $1.132$ and $1.287$ markers per 1,000
transcript tokens. Marker counts are 12, 54, and 40, over 23,296, 47,705,
and 31,087 tokens. The composition also changes: integration markers account
for 20 of 54 markers at $T_2$, while blockers account for 7 of 40 at $T_3$.
These are deterministic lexical matches in a global corpus, not diagnoses of
coordination failure; transcript coverage is unavailable for 2026.1.

\subsection{RQ3: How does the observable scope and content evidence of upfront architectural artifacts at baseline \texorpdfstring{$T_1$}{T1} associate with late-stage clean-path rework and independent, blind-scored project outcomes?}

RQ3 is evaluated at team-semester grain across only 14 observations. This
small sample substantially limits statistical power and the stability of any
correlation estimate; accordingly, the reported rank-based Spearman
coefficients are used only to summarize directional monotonic patterns in the
observed cases, not as confirmatory tests. This narrower analysis complements
the broader descriptive evidence for RQ1 and RQ2, which is situated in a cohort
of approximately 70 participating students and draws on repeated surveys,
repositories from all 14 projects, and the available transcript corpus. These
evidence streams retain their distinct
analytical grains and are not treated as 70 independent observations. The RQ3
analysis separates observable upfront architectural artifacts from their
semantic interpretation, separates rework magnitude from a
baseline-conditioned ratio, and treats evaluator outcomes as independent T3
scorecard summaries.

% notebooks are verify_m6.ipynb through verify_m9.ipynb.

Architectural Artifact Scope (M6a) records whether a T1 architectural
artifact was observed and its
repository-visible structural scope. An artifact is present in 4 of 9
team-semesters in 2025.2 and in all 5 team-semesters in 2026.1. Among the
2025.2 observations, the median T1 file count is zero and the median T1 line
delta is zero; among the 2026.1 observations, the corresponding medians are
4 files and 114 changed lines. The largest observed T1 line delta is 232 in
2025.2 and 222 in 2026.1. These are structural observations, not measures of
architectural quality or planning intent. Figure~\ref{fig:rq3-presence}
summarizes the cohort-level presence pattern.

file-provenance proxy for destructive rework. Among the 14 team-semesters,
12 are baseline-eligible. The T3 clean-rework ratio ranges from $0.000$ to

$1.000$ but very different absolute magnitudes ($172$ and $196$ lines).
Baseline-ineligible cases remain separate and are not interpreted as stable or
low-rework cases. Because path provenance does not establish semantic defects,
Clean-Path Rework (M8) is not a direct defect measure.

Overall, the RQ3 evidence provides a descriptive comparison of observable T1
artifact scope, late clean-path changes, and independent evaluator outcomes,
but the 14-team-semester sample cannot support stable confirmatory correlation
or establish that artifact quality causes less destructive rework or better
project outcomes. The principal result is therefore a bounded, exploratory
association profile with explicit missingness and influence diagnostics, not a
causal planning-effect estimate.
